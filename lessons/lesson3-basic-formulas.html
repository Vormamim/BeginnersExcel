Lesson 3: Basic Formulas

Learn how to make the spreadsheet do the maths for you by using simple formulas step by step.

Start Lesson

🎯 Learning Goals

Know that every formula starts with an equals sign
Use the multiply symbol to work out totals
Use SUM to add a group of numbers
Try AVERAGE, MAX, and MIN to learn more about your data

🧮 Step 1: What is a Formula?

A formula is something you type into a cell to make the spreadsheet calculate an answer for you.

Every formula must start with an equals sign (=). If you do not type the equals sign first, the spreadsheet will think you are just typing normal words.

Simple example

If you click a cell, type =2+2, and press Enter, the spreadsheet will show the answer 4.

✏️ Step 2: Learn the Maths Symbols

Spreadsheets use symbols instead of writing words like add or multiply.

Symbol What it means Example
+Add=5+3
-Subtract=10-4
*Multiply=6*7
/Divide=20/4
Important

The * symbol means multiply. You do not type an x.

🧪 Try It: Your First Formula

Click an empty cell.
Type =2+2.
Press Enter.
The answer should appear in the cell.

If you got 4, your formula worked.

📦 Step 3: Set Up Your Table

Before using formulas, you need some data in your spreadsheet.

Click cell A1 and type Item.
Click cell B1 and type Price.
Click cell C1 and type Quantity.
Click cell D1 and type Total.
Under those headings, type at least four rows of data.
Example row

You could type Apples in A2, 2 in B2, and 3 in C2.

🧮 Step 4: Multiply Price by Quantity

Now you are ready to make the spreadsheet work out the total for one row.

Click cell D2.
Type =B2*C2.
Press Enter.
The spreadsheet will multiply the number in B2 by the number in C2.
What this means

If B2 is 2 and C2 is 3, then =B2*C2 gives you 6.

🧪 Try It: Fill More Totals

Click cell D3.
Type =B3*C3.
Press Enter.
Do the same for D4 and D5.

Now each row should have its own total.

➕ Step 5: Use SUM

SUM adds a whole group of numbers for you.

Click the empty cell under your totals. For example, if your totals are in D2 to D5, click D6.
Type =SUM(D2:D5).
Press Enter.
The spreadsheet will add every number from D2 to D5.
What D2:D5 means

This is called a range. It means all the cells from D2 down to D5.

📊 Step 6: Try AVERAGE, MAX, and MIN

These formulas help you understand your numbers quickly.

Formula What it does Example
AVERAGEFinds the middle value=AVERAGE(D2:D5)
MAXFinds the biggest number=MAX(D2:D5)
MINFinds the smallest number=MIN(D2:D5)
Click an empty cell.
Type one of the formulas above.
Press Enter.
Look at the answer and compare it to your totals.
